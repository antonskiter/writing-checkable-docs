# Sentence rows

How a sentence reads, distilled to checks from Williams (*Style: Lessons in
Clarity and Grace*), with the stance from classic style (Thomas & Turner;
Pinker): prose is a window — the writer has seen something and positions the
reader to see it, not to watch the writer thinking. A sentence enters these
rows only after S1 hires it; clarity spent on an unhired sentence is waste.
Every W row is doc-side — no tree, no execution — and runs at every audit
level.

### W1 · Character

**Contract.** The grammatical subject names the actor of the sentence's
story — short, concrete, early.

**Check.** Name who or what the sentence is about; that name not the subject
fires. A subject running past about seven words fires. An abstraction in the
subject seat clears only when the abstraction itself is the doc's actor (the
build, the refusal), not a wrapper around one.

### W2 · Action

**Contract.** The action is the verb, not a noun made from one.

**Check.** Per nominalization, and per empty verb carrying one (make, do,
have, perform, provide, conduct): convert to the verb and compare — the verb
form wins unless the nominalization names a settled thing the doc treats as
an object (an installation, the configuration).

### W3 · Given before new

**Contract.** A sentence opens with what the reader already holds and ends
with its news; the stress position is the last words (Williams: cohesion,
old-to-new).

**Check.** Read each opening against the previous sentence: no link fires.
Read the last words: the news sitting mid-sentence while the tail trails off
in qualifiers fires — flip the sentence.

### W4 · Topic string

**Contract.** A paragraph's subjects form one short set; the paragraph is
about what sits in its subject seats.

**Check.** List the subject of every sentence in the paragraph. A scattered
list is an incoherent paragraph: re-seat the subjects or split the paragraph.

### W5 · Reach the verb

**Contract.** Subject and verb sit together, early; complexity hangs at the
end (end-weight).

**Check.** Count the words before the main verb and between subject and
verb. A long open or a long interruption moves to the tail or becomes its own
sentence.

### W6 · Every word works

**Contract.** A word earns its seat (Williams' concision: meaningless words,
doubled pairs, what the reader infers, a phrase a word can replace, a
negative an affirmative can replace, metadiscourse).

**Check.** Delete the word; meaning intact means it was dead. A hedge or an
intensifier survives only where it changes what the reader does next.

### W7 · One name per thing

**Contract.** One concept, one term, the doc through — and the term is the
one the system's own surfaces use. Elegant variation is a defect in a doc,
not grace.

**Check.** List the doc's names for each concept: two names for one thing
fires, keep the system's. One name for two things fires harder — split them.

### W8 · Instruction register

**Contract.** An instruction is imperative, present tense, addressed to the
reader; one action per step, the verb leading (the register the Google and
Microsoft style guides own).

**Check.** Per step: no leading verb, a tense other than present, or two
actions in one step fires. Prose describing what a command does is not an
instruction and W8 does not reach it.

## Sources

Williams owns W1–W6 (*Style*, the clarity, cohesion and concision lessons);
Pinker (*The Sense of Style*) grounds the stance and the curse of knowledge;
the Google Developer Documentation and Microsoft Writing style guides own the
register W7–W8 operationalize. Consulted for mechanisms; the checks above are
the working surface.
